# Opening framing

The most common reason a named shot comes back as the wrong shot. A camera move needs range, and the
opening framing is where the range comes from.

## The rule

**Open at whichever end of the range the subject is LARGEST.**

A move that must *end* tight has to *open* wide. A move that must end wide opens tight. State it as
framing in the `{FRAMING}` slot - *"An extreme wide shot looks straight down a forest track and
places `<Subject 1>` small and distant at the far end of it"*.

Two shots demonstrated it in the same scene on the same day, from the same default:

| | must end | opened on | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aerial pullback** | wide | the default waist-up medium close-up | ✅ full figure to a speck on the track |
| **Super dolly in** | tight | the same waist-up default | ❌ *"not a super dolly at all"* - nowhere to travel, read as a zoom |

One default: correct for one shot, fatal for the other. When it was given an explicit extreme-wide
opening, its subject range went from 3.08x to 7.42x.

## The exception

**Shots where the subject stays locked open where they end**, because their scale never changes.
Dolly zoom and snorricam both belong here. A dolly zoom given a wide opening became a plain push-in.

## Ask for framing, not for a lens

A wide **lens** does nothing. Measured: an 8mm produced framing pixel-for-pixel identical to a 50mm
and to naming no lens at all. A 75mm did render tighter, so focal length is asymmetric - long is in
distribution, ultra-wide is not - but neither is a reliable lever.

**So a wide shot has to be asked for as a wide shot.** Two shots in this library needed their
framing overridden explicitly where a focal length had been expected to do the work: the super dolly
in needed the extreme wide opening, and the snorricam needed an explicit tight crop rather than a
14mm.

## The unresolved case

The **eyes-in** shot set no framing override and **opened wider than the default anyway**, ending on
an iris filling the frame. A sufficiently extreme destination may widen the opening by itself.

⚠️ n=1. If it generalises it softens this whole rule. Worth one deliberate test before relying on it.
